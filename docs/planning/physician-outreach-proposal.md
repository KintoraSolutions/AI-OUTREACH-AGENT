## 1. Objective

We want to redesign our existing clinic outreach system to improve our ability to reach the individuals who actually make purchasing and operational decisions within medical clinics.

Rather than relying on generic clinic email addresses, the new system should identify key decision-makers, research their professional backgrounds and clinic affiliations, and develop a more targeted, personalized outreach strategy.

This redesign will be implemented through a **multi-phase development process**, with separate workflows and specialized agents responsible for research, lead qualification, contact discovery, and outreach.

## 2. Current Workflow

Our existing outreach system operates as follows:

1. **Clinic Discovery:** Research and identify potential clinics.
2. **Clinic Evaluation:** Score clinics using user-defined criteria to determine their suitability.
3. **Lead Selection:** Allow the user to review and select which clinics to contact.
4. **LLM Integration:** Send relevant clinic information to an LLM through an API.
5. **Email Generation:** Generate a personalized outreach email.
6. **Human Approval:** Present the draft to the user for review, approval, and sending.

## 3. Problems With the Existing Workflow

Although the current system successfully identifies clinics and generates outreach emails, it has several limitations that reduce its effectiveness.

**Problem 1: Emails Are Not Reaching Decision-Makers**

Generic clinic email addresses are frequently monitored by receptionists, administrative assistants, or other front-desk staff rather than clinic owners, medical directors, or senior physicians.

Consequently, our outreach often fails to reach individuals with the authority to evaluate or purchase our services.

**Problem 2: Clinic Inboxes Are Overcrowded**

General clinic inboxes receive high volumes of patient inquiries, appointment requests, prescription-related messages, and administrative communications.

Business outreach emails can easily become buried, ignored, or filtered out before reaching the appropriate person.

**Problem 3: Outreach Is Not Targeted Enough**

Our current email generation process primarily personalizes messages around the clinic itself, without adequately considering the specific individual receiving the message.

This limits our ability to create compelling, relevant outreach that directly addresses the recipient's professional responsibilities, business interests, and operational challenges.

## 4. Proposed Workflow: Decision-Maker Discovery and Targeted Outreach

We need to introduce a new branch into our existing outreach architecture that focuses on identifying and contacting the physicians and business leaders associated with each clinic.

The proposed workflow is:

### Step 1: Research the Clinic

Conduct a deeper analysis of each clinic's website and publicly available information.

Identify its services, locations, medical team, ownership structure where available, and any other information relevant to understanding its operations.

### Step 2: Identify Key Physicians and Decision-Makers

Analyze the clinic's website and other publicly available professional sources to identify:

- Clinic owners and founding physicians
- Medical directors and lead physicians
- Physicians with operational or administrative leadership roles
- Individuals associated with multiple clinics or larger medical organizations

The goal is to determine who has meaningful influence over operational and purchasing decisions.

### Step 3: Research Physician Affiliations

Once key physicians have been identified, conduct additional research to understand their professional networks and organizational involvement.

The system should identify which clinics they are affiliated with, whether they own or manage multiple locations, and whether they have relationships with broader healthcare organizations.

This creates an opportunity to identify high-value leads who could potentially introduce our services across multiple clinics.

### Step 4: Discover and Verify Direct Contact Information

Introduce a dedicated **Physician Contact Discovery Branch** that operates independently from our existing general clinic email discovery process.

This branch should search for publicly available professional contact information associated with each identified decision-maker.

Where possible, it should determine:

- Direct professional email addresses
- Publicly listed business contact channels
- Professional profiles and organizational affiliations
- The reliability and source of each contact

Contact information should be verified and assigned a confidence score. The system should not invent or assume email addresses when reliable information is unavailable.

### Step 5: Evaluate and Rank Decision-Makers

Expand our existing scoring mechanism to evaluate both clinics and individual contacts.

The user should be able to define scoring criteria based on factors such as:

- Decision-making authority
- Number of affiliated clinics
- Clinic size and operational complexity
- Relevance to our product
- Contact information reliability
- Potential commercial value

This will allow us to prioritize not only the most promising clinics, but also the most valuable individuals within those organizations.

### Step 6: Generate Personalized Outreach

Use the LLM API to generate outreach emails tailored to the individual recipient.

Rather than sending a generic introduction to the clinic, each email should reflect the physician's professional role, relevant clinic affiliations, and the specific operational problems our product can address.

Emails should be concise, compelling, and designed to establish a clear reason for the recipient to respond.

### Step 7: User Review and Approval

Maintain a human-in-the-loop approval process.

The user should be able to review the identified decision-maker, supporting research, contact confidence, clinic affiliations, and generated email before deciding whether to send it.

No outreach should be sent without explicit user authorization.

## 5. Multi-Phase Implementation Strategy

Rather than rebuilding the entire system simultaneously, we should introduce these capabilities incrementally.

**Phase 1 — Existing System Assessment**

Review the current clinic discovery, scoring, and email generation architecture. Identify reusable components and determine where the new decision-maker discovery branch should be integrated.

**Phase 2 — Physician Identification Agent**

Build a dedicated research agent that identifies lead physicians, clinic owners, medical directors, and other relevant decision-makers.

**Phase 3 — Physician Affiliation Mapping**

Develop a research process that connects physicians to their associated clinics, ownership interests where publicly documented, and professional networks.

**Phase 4 — Professional Contact Discovery Agent**

Implement a separate discovery and verification system for physician-level professional email addresses and other appropriate business contact channels.

**Phase 5 — Enhanced Lead Scoring**

Expand the existing scoring system to evaluate individual decision-makers alongside clinics, using user-configurable criteria.

**Phase 6 — Personalized Outreach Generation**

Improve the LLM email generation process so that its messaging is based on verified, recipient-specific research rather than generic clinic information.

**Phase 7 — User Approval and Outreach Management**

Integrate the new research and outreach branches into a unified workflow that allows users to review leads, approve drafts, send emails, and track outreach history.

**Phase 8 — Testing, Optimization, and Deployment**

Test each new component independently before enabling the complete workflow. Monitor contact discovery accuracy, email delivery, response rates, and conversion performance to identify opportunities for improvement.

## 6. Desired End State

The ultimate objective is to transition from a **clinic-based email outreach system** into an **intelligent, decision-maker-focused lead generation platform**.

The system should no longer stop after identifying a promising clinic. Instead, it should determine who is responsible for that clinic, identify other clinics that person may influence, locate reliable professional contact information, and generate highly personalized outreach.

The redesigned system should retain the existing clinic discovery capabilities while introducing specialized research branches that operate together through a structured, multi-phase architecture.

**The core shift is simple: Instead of finding clinics and emailing their general inboxes, we want to find the people who make decisions for those clinics and reach them directly.**
